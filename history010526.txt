The logic behind these three core metrics ensures that your plant operates like a synchronized clock rather than a "greedy" system that moves whenever it's free. Here is the technical breakdown of how they are calculated in `scheduler.py`:

### 1. Cycle Time (Stagger)
The **Cycle Time** (or Stagger) determines how often a new load can enter the plant. It is calculated by finding the **Master Bottleneck** of the entire sequence:

*   **For Single Tanks:** The bottleneck is the `Target Dip Time` + the `Travel Time` to the next station.
*   **For Parallel/Alternating Tanks (e.g., Zinc Phosphating):** Since you have two tanks, they share the load. The bottleneck is calculated as `(Dip Time + Travel Time) / 2`.
*   **The Formula:** 
    $$\text{Target Cycle Time} = \max(\text{Process Bottleneck}_1, \text{Process Bottleneck}_2, \dots)$$
    *In your current setup, the Zinc Phosphating tanks allow for a cycle time of approx **391 seconds**.*

---

### 2. Processing Time (Dip Time)
The **Processing Time** is the duration a load sits inside a tank. However, to maintain the repetitive cycle, the scheduler uses **Uniform Dip Logic**:

*   **Target vs. Minimum:** The scheduler takes your "Target Dip" from the CSV but may add "Slack Time" to it.
*   **Slack Distribution:** If a station (like SOAK) is not the bottleneck, the wagon might be free earlier than needed. To keep the cycle repetitive, the scheduler calculates exactly how much extra time the load should stay in the tank to hit the master 391s stagger.
*   **Criticality Rules:** Slack is added to **LOW** criticality tanks first. 
    *   *Logic:* `Actual Processing Time = User_Target_Dip + Slack_to_hit_Cycle_Stagger`.

---

### 3. Accumulated Time
The **Accumulated Time** is the running "Wall Clock" for the physical transporter (Wagon). It starts at **0** and is strictly additive:

*   **Movement:** Every time the wagon moves, it adds `(Distance / Speed) + Lift Time + Lower Time`.
*   **Waiting:** If the wagon arrives at a tank before the load has finished its "Processing Time," it calculates a `Wait for sec` value. 
    *   *Logic:* `Wait = Ready_Time - Arrival_Time`. 
    *   This wait is added to the **Accumulated Time**.
*   **Sequence:** In your `OUTPUT_sequence.csv`, the `ACCUMULATED TIME` column tells the PLC exactly what second each instruction should start relative to the very beginning of the program.
